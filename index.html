Recognize the Signs

Are You Experiencing These Symptoms?

Diabetic neuropathy symptoms often start gradually. Recognizing these signs early can prevent long-term complications and severe nerve damage.

Burning Feet

A constant or intermittent hot, burning sensation in the toes and soles of feet.

Learn More

Tingling Sensation

Feeling of 'pins and needles' that can become painful or distracting over time.

Learn More

Foot Numbness

Loss of sensation in feet, making it difficult to feel small injuries or cuts.

Learn More

Sharp Nerve Pain

Sudden, electric shock-like pains that shoot through legs and feet.

Learn More

Weakness in Legs

Difficulty walking or a feeling of instability due to muscle weakness.

Learn More

Sensitivity Problems

Extreme sensitivity to touch, where even bed sheets can feel painful.

Diabetic neuropathy is a type of nerve damage that can occur if you have diabetes. High blood sugar (glucose) can injure nerves throughout your body, but it most often damages nerves in your legs and feet.
While it was once thought that nerve damage was permanent, many modern treatments and tight glycemic control can significantly improve symptoms, prevent further damage, and in some cases, help nerves recover some function.
In the context of diabetes, burning feet are often caused by damage to the small nerve fibers that transmit pain and temperature sensations. This is a classic symptom of peripheral neuropathy.
No, most neuropathy treatments are non-invasive and painless. Our goal is to reduce your pain, not add to it. We use gentle diagnostic tools and non-surgical therapies.
Treatment is often a journey rather than a single event. While some patients feel relief within weeks, significant stabilization and improvement usually happen over 3-6 months of consistent care.
If left untreated for a long period, nerve damage can become permanent. That is why early diagnosis and aggressive management of both diabetes and neuropathy symptoms are absolutely critical.
